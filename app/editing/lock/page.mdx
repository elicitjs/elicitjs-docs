# Locked rows (read-only data)

<Lead>`lock` on the spec makes **given** rows read-only: the record so far, points already measured, last quarter’s actuals. No gesture can change or delete a locked row. Rows an edit _adds_ stay editable. Locked rows are still part of `getData()`; to leave given rows out of the answer, use [`context`](/editing/existence#context).</Lead>

<ApiReference entries={api} />

<Section id="seed" title="Locked seed, free additions">

`lock: "seed"` locks exactly the rows you passed as `data`. Rows a `create` mints afterwards are ordinary, editable data. One dataset can hold the observed points and your answer.

<Example
  code={scatterObservedPointsYours}
  title="Scatter: observed points + yours"
  blurb="The five grey points are the seed — try to drag one and nothing happens. Click to add your own, and those you can move and delete."

/>

</Section>

<Section id="predicate" title="Locking rows by what they are">

Pass a predicate to lock rows by their fields. Here the quarters that already happened are actuals, and the rest are your forecast. Both live in one dataset and one bar mark.

<Example
  code={actualsForecast}
  title="Actuals + forecast"
  blurb={"lock: (d) => d.kind === \"actual\". The two grey bars are reported; only the forecast bars take a drag."}
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> Q3 / Q4 (blue). Q1 and Q2 are reported — they don’t budge.
    </>
  )}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="you-draw-it" title="You draw it">

This recreates the New York Times’ “You Draw It” (2017): the record runs to 1990 and you draw the rest. Both halves use the same dataset, line mark and `y` field; only the lock differs. Proximity picks skip locked rows, so a drag beside the record starts drawing instead of grabbing it.

`edit.line.draw({ stage: 0 })` draws the missing years in stage 0. `chart.nextStage()` moves to stage 1, which retires the edit and shows the answer key.

<Example
  code={drawTheLineForTheMissingYears}
  title="Draw the line for the missing years"
  blurb={"edit.line.draw({ samples: years }) paints one point per year as the pointer crosses its column. Drag back over the record and it will not budge — the lock repairs those rows and keeps the rest of the stroke."}
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> left-to-right across the empty half to draw 1991–2016, then <b>drag again</b> to refine it. Then press <b>Show me how I did</b>.
    </>
  )}
/>

</Section>
