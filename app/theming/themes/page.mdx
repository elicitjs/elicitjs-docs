# Named themes

<Lead>A **named theme** restyles a whole chart from one word: `theme: "nyt"`. There are eighteen. Fourteen are the house styles of [Flint](https://github.com/microsoft/flint-chart), Microsoft's chart grammar, transcribed from its presets; `default`, `survey`, `dark` and `dd` are Elicit.js's own. Each is a partial [theme](/theming), so a chart can start from one and change any token.</Lead>

<Section id="by-name" title="Using a named theme">

Pass the name as `theme`, or the object from `themes`. `theme: "nyt"` and `theme: themes.nyt` are the same. `setTheme("nyt")` makes it the default for every chart built afterwards.

A named theme sets the surface, the ink and palettes, the fonts of the labels and the title, which axes draw a spine and ticks, which get gridlines, the zero line, the band padding, corner radii, line widths, point size, and the colour of the hover and selection outline. Anything you place yourself stays where you put it.

<Example
  code={byName}
  title="theme: &quot;nyt&quot;"
  blurb="A serif headline over sans-serif labels, no value-axis spine, a quiet grid and a rule at zero."
  try={"drag a dot after 2024."}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="newsroom" title="Newsroom">

`nyt`, `economist`, `bbc` and `datawrapper` are editorial styles. They drop the value-axis spine and ticks, keep a light value grid, and draw a rule at zero. `nyt` sets its headline in Georgia. `economist` uses a blue-grey grid and tighter bars. `bbc` uses large type, a four-colour palette and a heavy zero rule. `datawrapper` uses Roboto and a light solid grid.

<Example
  code={newsroom}
  title="nyt · economist · bbc · datawrapper"
  blurb="The same grouped bar chart under each newsroom theme."
  try={"drag any bar — every theme keeps the same gesture."}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="print" title="Print and science">

`swiss` is the International Typographic Style: warm paper, black spines, short outward ticks on the value axis, one signal red. `nature` is the journal figure: black Arial, full spines with long ticks, no gridlines, and the Okabe–Ito colours. `deStijl` uses red, yellow and blue on off-white, heavy black value gridlines, and an italic Futura title.

<Example
  code={print}
  title="swiss · nature · deStijl"
  blurb="Typographic, scientific and Neo-Plastic."
  try={"drag a bar."}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="business" title="Business">

`mckinsey` draws no axes and no gridlines, a near-black primary over electric blues, and a baseline. `powerbiLight` and `powerbi` are the dashboard tile on a white and a near-black canvas: Segoe UI, a quiet grid, bars rounded by 3px. The dark tile's sequential ramp runs from dim to bright, so a higher value is brighter.

<Example
  code={business}
  title="mckinsey · powerbiLight · powerbi"
  blurb="Consulting deck and dashboard tiles."
  try={"drag a bar."}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="product" title="Product interfaces">

`apple` uses the system colours, no spines or ticks, a hairline grid and bars rounded by 4px. `material` is Material Design 3: a purple-led tonal palette, Google Sans and Roboto, outline-variant gridlines, rounded bars and round legend chips.

<Example
  code={product}
  title="apple · material"
  blurb="Quiet app-interface charts."

/>

</Section>

<Section id="playful" title="Playful">

`pop` extends `swiss`: process colours on yellow, heavy black spines, thick outlines on every filled mark, oversized points and Arial Black. `cartoon` uses cream paper, a comic typeface, dark outlines, rounded bars, chunky lines and a dashed grid.

<Section id="own" title="Elicit.js's own">

`default` is the built-in look. `survey` restyles the [survey widgets](/widgets) with an indigo accent and Inter. `dark` paints its own slate surface, so it reads on a dark page with no host CSS. `dd` is the Data & Dialogue Lab theme: the lab's teal as the ink, its blue for handles and answers, then coral, gold, violet, leaf and rose for categories. Shapes carry no stroke, and a stroke that does appear is the same hue a step darker. Every chart on this site is drawn with it.

The palette is also available as colour schemes: `scheme: "dd"` for categories, `"ddramp"` for a sequential channel and `"dddiverging"` for a diverging one.

<Section id="extends" title="Extending a theme">

`extends` starts a partial theme from a named one, and the rest of the object deep-merges over it. The name comes from Flint. Use it to keep a house style and change its colour, a radius or a font.

<Example
  code={extendsTheme}
  title="{ extends: &quot;swiss&quot;, … }"
  blurb="Swiss, with a blue ink, rounded bars and an italic title."
  try={"drag a bar."}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="flint" title="What carries over from Flint">

Each preset keeps Flint's colours, font stacks, weights and proportions. Flint's `bandFraction` becomes `bandPadding` (one minus it), a point's area becomes a radius, and its presence words become tokens: `omit` turns a spine off or sets `tickSize: 0`, and a value-axis grid becomes `grid.show: "continuous"`. Flint names type sizes with tokens (`text.100` to `text.500`), which these themes set as 11 to 24 px.

Some parts of a Flint theme are layout, which Elicit.js leaves to the spec: data labels on marks, series names at line ends, a value axis on the right, and furniture such as The Economist's red tab or the BBC's footer rule. Draw those with marks and [guides](/guides) where you need them.

Flint is MIT-licensed. Its theme specification is at [docs/theme-spec.md](https://github.com/microsoft/flint-chart/blob/main/docs/theme-spec.md), and its theme gallery at [microsoft.github.io/flint-chart](https://microsoft.github.io/flint-chart/#/themes).

</Section>
