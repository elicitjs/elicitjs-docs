# Scales & channels

<Lead>Two different words, kept apart. A **data type** says what a field _is_ — `quantitative`, `categorical`, `ordinal`, `temporal` — and is declared once, on the [schema](/schema). A **scale type** says how a channel _draws_ it — `linear`, `log`, `band`, `ordinal`, … — and is normally **derived**: a categorical field on a bar’s x is a band (a bar needs the interval), on a dot’s x it is a point (a dot wants the tick). So you rarely name a scale at all. When you do, `scale` takes a name, a spec, or a live d3 scale. Every positional scale is invertible — that is what makes editing possible.</Lead>

<Section id="zero-crossing" title="Crossing zero — symlog & diverging">

Elicited quantities are often _differences_: a revision, an error, a surprise. Those span orders of magnitude and go both ways, which breaks the usual two tools — `log` has nothing to say about 0 or −60, and a `sequential` ramp has no reference point. `symlog` is linear near zero and logarithmic beyond it; `diverging` colours by distance from a pivot, each side scaled on its own.

<Section id="ordered-colour" title="Ordered data gets an ordered ramp">

Colour follows the field's **measure**, not just its scale. `categorical` values are unordered, so they get distinct hues. `ordinal` values run low to high, so they get a **ramp sampled to one swatch per category** — light to dark, in order. A Likert scale reads as a scale rather than as four unrelated colours.

Declare the difference and the colour follows:

```js
agree: { type: "ordinal",     domain: ["disagree", "neutral", "agree", "strongly agree"] }
team:  { type: "categorical", domain: ["north", "south", "east"] }
```

Use `categorical` for a group identifier even when you happen to list it in an order — a series key is not an ordered scale.

</Section>

<Section id="two-encodings-colour" title="Two encodings on one channel">

A scale belongs to one **encoding** — a (channel, field) pair — so two marks colouring by *different* fields get two scales, each with its own domain and its own legend. They also get different scheme **families**, so the two are perceptually distinct rather than sharing one palette's slots.

Two marks colouring by the *same* field share one scale, one set of colours, and one legend: that is what makes a shared colour mean a shared value.

</Section>

<Section id="categorical-position" title="Categorical position → point scale">

When a positional channel is categorical, a dot resolves it to a point scale — the mark sits on the category’s tick. Two categorical axes make a discrete grid. Note `ordinal` vs `categorical` on `y`: an ordinal domain’s _order_ is meaningful.
