# Three new glyphs

<Lead>Three elicitation interfaces built from existing marks and edits: a face whose colour you slide, a plant you grow, and an hourglass you fill. Each follows the same steps: declare the fields, draw the parts, then put an edit on each channel a gesture should write.</Lead>

<Section id="steps" title="The steps">

1. **Declare the answer.** One field per thing you ask. The `schema` gives each field its type and domain.
2. **Draw the glyph.** A [`composite` in box mode](/marks/composite#box) places every part in the glyph's own units: `x` and `y` from `-1` to `1`, y up. The composite's `size` sets the box's half-size, so the whole glyph scales with it.
3. **Bind the fields.** A channel with a `field` and a `frame: [lo, hi]` range maps the field's domain onto that slice of the box. Two parts that read the same field and range always line up.
4. **Add the gestures.** An edit on a channel writes that channel's field: `move` for a position, `slide` for a size, colour or angle. A part with no edit can't be grabbed, so a drag passes through it.

</Section>

<Section id="face" title="A face that changes colour">

[`face()`](/marks/face) already draws a head, eyes, brows and a mouth. Its `fill` channel colours the head, so binding it to a field adds a question with no new parts.

`feeling` runs from `-1` (anger) to `1` (disgust). A diverging scale maps it from red through yellow to green. `slide({ axis: "x" })` on the head writes it, so you drag the head left for anger and right for disgust. `slide` works on a colour ramp because it moves along the domain rather than inverting a colour. The mouth keeps its own `slide` on valence, so the two answers come from two different parts.

</Section>

<Section id="plant" title="A plant that grows">

Three answers about your week, one plant: `energy` is how tall it grows, `joy` how big it blooms, and `support` how full its leaves are.

The stem's top and the flower both read `energy` over the same range, `frame: [-0.15, 0.65]`, so the flower always sits on the stem. Drag the flower up or down: `move({ channels: ["y"] })` writes `energy`. Drag it sideways: a `slide` on its `size` writes `joy`. The two edits read different directions of one drag, so they don't interfere.

Each leaf's centre and width both read `support`. As the leaf widens, its centre moves out by the same amount, so its inner edge stays on the stem. Drag a leaf outward to make it fuller.

</Section>

<Section id="hourglass" title="An hourglass you fill">

How long will this work take? The timer holds 12 weeks of sand. The top bulb holds your estimate and the bottom holds the rest.

Each pile of sand is a `rect`, drawn as a plain bar. The top pile's upper edge, `y2`, reads `weeks` over `frame: [0, 0.8]`. The bottom pile reads the same field over the mirrored range, `[0, -0.8]`, so as the top drains the bottom fills. Both carry `move({ mode: "relative" })`, so you can drag either pile and its surface stays under the pointer. `snap({ step: 1 })` rounds the answer to whole weeks.

The glass is a [`shape`](/marks/shape) drawn from an SVG path. A second `shape` is a mask: a box with the glass cut out of it, painted over the sand in the background colour. It trims each bar to the curve of its bulb. Neither shape carries an edit, so a drag passes through them to the sand.

<Example
  code={hourglass}
  title="How long will this take?"
  blurb="Two bars on one field with mirrored ranges, trimmed to the glass by a masking shape and snapped to whole weeks."
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag either pile of sand</b> up or down: the other pile moves the opposite way.
    </>
  )}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="positions" title="Glyphs as points">

A glyph's own `x` and `y` place it on the chart's scales, like a `point`. Give the composite a field for each, and you get one glyph per row: a scatter plot when both are quantitative, a grid when both are categories. The positions are data you already have, and the glyphs are the answers. Every part keeps its edit, so each glyph is answered on its own.

With `x` and `y` on band scales, each glyph fills its cell by default. On a continuous scale, set `size` to fix the box's half-size in pixels.

A `move` on the composite's `x` moves the whole glyph, writing both position fields. Its press would compete with the parts' edits, so `when` splits the gestures by modifier:

- `move({ channels: ["x", "y"], pick: "nearest", when: when.shift })` takes a Shift-drag and moves the nearest glyph.
- Every part's own edit takes `when: when.noShift`, so a plain drag still answers with the part you grab.

On a categorical axis the move snaps to the nearest category, so a plant dragged with Shift lands in another cell.
