# Ellipse

<Lead>`ellipse` is a dot with **two independent radii**. A `point` has one `size` channel because a circle has one radius; splitting it into `rx` and `ry` means each is its own magnitude channel — each can carry its own field, and each can carry its own edit. A shape that widens on one drag and flattens on another is **two elicited numbers in one mark**.</Lead>

<ApiReference entries={api} />

<Section id="uncertainty" title="Two uncertainties, one blob">

The classic use is an estimate with per-axis uncertainty: the centre is what someone believes, and the two radii are how sure they are along each axis separately. A circle can only say "unsure overall"; this can say "confident about cost, vague about benefit".

Both radius edits live on the same mark, so a single diagonal drag reshapes the blob. That's fine here — `slide({ axis: 'x' })` and `slide({ axis: 'y' })` read different components of the gesture, so neither fights the other.

<Section id="movable" title="Moving it as well">

Two edits that read the *same* component of a drag can't share one mark — one gesture would fire both, and a `move()` on the centre would fight a radius `slide`. Give them a mark each with a [composite](/marks/composite): an `ellipse` that resizes, and a `point` at its centre that moves. Direct-pick routes a gesture to the mark you touched, so nothing has to arbitrate.

<Section id="glyphs" title="Inside a glyph">

`ellipse` is what a [face](/marks/face)'s eyes are: an `rx` that opens and an `ry` that squints, both stated as fractions of the glyph's frame so they scale with the head. See [composite](/marks/composite#box) for how a mark places itself in a glyph's local coordinates.
