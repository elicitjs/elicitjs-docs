# Curve

<Lead>`curve` draws a segment bowed off its chord. The bend is the `curvature` channel, so how much it bends, and which way, is an elicited number. `curveY` spans `x` and sits on `y`, `curveX` spans `y` and sits on `x`, and `curve` reads the channels you declare.</Lead>

<ApiReference entries={api} />

<Section id="bend" title="One number: how much it bends">

`curvature` is the apex's offset from the chord as a fraction of the **half-chord**. 0 is a straight segment, and ±1 bows out by half the span's length. The shape stays proportional when the span changes, so a face's mouth keeps its expression when the head is resized.

Positive curvature bows to the left of travel, from the first endpoint to the second, so a rightward chord bows up. Set the direction on the scale: a descending `range` turns a rising field into a deepening cup.

<Section id="tilt" title="Bend and tilt">

`angle` tilts the whole curve about its chord midpoint.

Both edits work on the same mark because they read different components of one drag: `slide({ axis: "y" })` on `curvature` and `slide({ axis: "x" })` on `angle`. Two edits that read the same component conflict, unless `when` gates one of them (see [Shape](/marks/shape#path)). Use `slide`, not `rotate`, on `angle`: you grab the curve at its own pivot, so a few pixels of travel swing `rotate` through most of a half-turn and pin the value to an extreme.

<Section id="grabbing" title="Grabbing a curve">

A curve carries a wide transparent hit area along its stroke, so a drag near the line catches it, on both SVG and canvas. Edits measure from the chord midpoint. A curve with no edit lets drags pass through to the marks beneath it.
