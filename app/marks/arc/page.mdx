<Section id="edit" title="Editable slice boundaries">

Wire `edit.stack.edge()` and every **interior** boundary gets a draggable handle, so _n_ slices give _n_ − 1 handles. The seam where the last slice meets the first is the layout's fixed anchor: with the orientation and the total both fixed, those _n_ − 1 boundaries are all that can move independently.

Dragging one moves value between exactly the two slices it separates. One grows by what the other loses, so the total is preserved.

<Section id="slice" title="Cutting and merging slices">

A pie divides one total among its rows, which is what a stacked `bar` does along an axis — so the same three gestures work on both:

- `edit.stack.cut()` — **click** inside a slice to divide it in two at the pointer angle.
- `edit.stack.edge()` — **drag** a boundary to move value across it.
- `edit.stack.merge()` — **double-click** a boundary to fuse those two slices back into one.

Each preserves the pie's total. A cut takes its category from the schema's domain, so it needs no typing: a closed domain hands out the values this pie doesn't yet use and refuses the cut once they run out, while `open: true` lets each cut append a new one. See [Slicing a stack](/marks/bar#slice) for the bar form.

<Section id="grid" title="A grid of donuts">

A donut is a view over a **group** of rows the way a `face` is a view over one row — so it scatters into the plane the same way. Bind `x` (and optionally `y`) to a field and each distinct value becomes a **slot** holding its own donut; the rows at that slot are its slices. There is no group transform — the **encoding is the grouping**. (A `size` field scales each donut independently; `outerRadius` pins one radius for all; otherwise the radius fits the slot.)

Here `x: { field: 'state' }` gives a band axis, so `IL` / `NY` / `NC` each get a donut of their own party split. A boundary drag redistributes share **within that state only** — the per-slot sum a pie holds by construction — and never disturbs another state.
