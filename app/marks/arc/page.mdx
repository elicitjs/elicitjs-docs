# Arc · Pie · Donut

<Lead>`arc` and `donut` draw **pie** and **donut** slices. Each row's magnitude, on the `theta` channel, is stacked and normalised to a full circle or a partial arc. `edit.stack.edge()`, `cut()` and `merge()` reshape the slices while keeping the total fixed. `x`/`y` scatter one donut per value, and a `radius` channel gives each slice its own rim for a rose plot.</Lead>

<Section id="edit" title="Editable slice boundaries">

Add `edit.stack.edge()` and every interior boundary gets a draggable handle, so _n_ slices give _n_ − 1 handles. The seam where the last slice meets the first stays fixed, since the orientation and the total are both fixed.

Dragging a boundary moves value between the two slices it separates. One grows by what the other loses, so the total is preserved.

<Section id="slice" title="Cutting and merging slices">

A pie divides one total among its rows, as a stacked `bar` does along an axis. The same three gestures work on both:

- `edit.stack.cut()` — **click** inside a slice to divide it in two at the pointer angle.
- `edit.stack.edge()` — **drag** a boundary to move value across it.
- `edit.stack.merge()` — **double-click** a boundary to fuse those two slices back into one.

Each preserves the pie's total. A cut needs a `stack` channel: it writes the new slice's category into that field, taking a value from its schema domain, so it needs no typing. A pie always stacks, so on `arc` the `stack` channel only names the slices and orders them by its domain. Without it, slices follow data order. A closed domain hands out the values this pie doesn't yet use, and refuses the cut once they run out. `open: true` lets each cut append a new one. See [Slicing a stack](/marks/bar#slice) for the bar form.

<Section id="grid" title="A grid of donuts">

Bind `x` (and optionally `y`) to a field and each distinct value becomes a **slot** with its own donut. The rows at that slot are its slices, so the encoding does the grouping. A `size` field scales each donut separately, and `outerRadius` sets one radius for all. Otherwise the radius fits the slot.

Here `x: { field: 'state' }` gives a band axis, so `IL`, `NY` and `NC` each get a donut of their own party split. A boundary drag moves share within that state only, and the other states keep their totals.

<Section id="radius" title="Rose plots (radius)">

The `radius` channel gives each slice its own outer radius, between `innerRadius` and `outerRadius`, for a rose or radial plot. `theta` still sets each slice's angle. `theta: { value: 1 }` makes every slice the same width, so the value reads from the rim alone. With no `outerRadius`, the ring's extent is 40% of the smaller plot dimension.

`scale: 'sqrt'` makes a slice's area grow with its value; the default linear scale makes its radius grow with it. Put `edit: move()` on the channel to drag a slice's rim in or out. `axisRadial({ channel: 'radius' })` draws concentric rings as its key.

<Example
  code={aRoseYouCanReshape}
  title="A rose you can reshape"
  blurb={"radius: { field: \"speed\", scale: \"sqrt\", edit: move() } — eight equal slices, each rim a value."}
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> a slice's rim outward or inward to change its speed.
    </>
  )}
/>

A `radius` channel replaces a per-donut `size` and sets one ring's extent. On a grid of donuts, every slot uses that extent.

</Section>
