# Constraints

<Lead>A constraint is a rule over the dataset, so it applies to every edit on every mark. It can reject a proposed change, or repair it by returning corrected rows. Every mark reads the same rows, so a repair shows up everywhere on the next render. Declare constraints in `constraints: [...]` on the `Elicit` spec, or on `Elicit.dataset` for [several charts](/dataset). `clamp` and `snap` bound a field, `maintainSum` fixes a total, `count` caps the number of rows, and `unique` forbids duplicate keys. `ordering` keeps a row's fields in order (lo ≤ mean ≤ hi), `monotonic` keeps a cumulative curve from dipping, and `spacing` keeps neighbours apart. `follow` and `symmetric` couple a range to its centre. `constraints.custom` defines your own.</Lead>

<ApiReference entries={api} />

<Section id="sum" title="maintainSum — a total held at 100">

`maintainSum` holds the total for every edit on every mark. Drag one bar and the others give way to keep the total at 100. `guide: true` draws the live bound.

<Example
  code={barsThatCompensate}
  title="Bars that compensate"
  blurb="maintainSum bounds the touched bar so the total stays 100; a rule guide marks the even split."
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> any bar — the others compensate.
    </>
  )}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="clamp" title="clamp — bound a field">

`clamp` holds a field inside `[min, max]`. It bounds the field you name, or the column the edit writes when you name none. An omitted bound falls back to the field's declared domain. `field` also takes a list, so one `clamp` can bound every column of a glyph. Pair it with a nearest pick and `guide: true` to show the bounds while you drag.

`clamp` and `snap` repair every cell the proposal changed. A neighbour that `ordering` or `monotonic` pushed is bounded too. Both rows of a stack edge land on the grid together, so the stack keeps its total. `snap` keeps a temporal field a `Date`, and its `step` is in milliseconds.

<Example
  code={clampedDragWithAGuide}
  title="Clamped drag with a guide"
  blurb="The y edit is clamped to 0–90; guide: true draws the bounds and the snap ring."
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> from anywhere in a column (clamped to 0–90).
    </>
  )}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="unique" title="unique — one per key">

`unique` forbids two rows with the same key. A single field guards one axis. An array is a composite key: at most one mark per cell, for both create and move.

/>

<Example
  code={compositeKeyABandBandGrid}

</Section>

<Section id="ordering" title="ordering — fields of a row, in order">

An error bar's caps and mean are separate marks over separate fields of one row. `ordering` keeps those fields in order across all three marks, whichever handle you drag. A low cap dragged above the high one would otherwise turn the interval inside-out.

<Example
  code={anIntervalThatStaysInOrder}
  title="An interval that can’t invert"
  blurb={"ordering({ field: [\"lo\", \"mean\", \"hi\"] }) — one rule across three marks."}
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> the low cap up past the mean: the mean and the high cap move up with it.
    </>
  )}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="follow" title="follow and symmetric — a range that moves with its mean">

`follow({ field: ['lo', 'hi'], lead: 'mean' })` moves the listed columns by the amount the `lead` column moved. Drag the mean and the whole interval travels with it. Drag an end and only that end moves, because `follow` reacts to the lead alone. The handles are plain `move()` edits.

<Example
  code={aRangeThatFollowsItsMean}
  title="A range that follows its mean"
  blurb={"follow({ field: [\"lo\", \"hi\"], lead: \"mean\" }) — the ends move with the mean, and on their own."}
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> the mean: the interval keeps its width and shape. <b>Drag</b> a cap: that end moves alone, so the interval can be lopsided.
    </>
  )}
/>

`symmetric({ field: ['lo', 'hi'], about: 'mean' })` mirrors the pair about the centre. Drag one end and the other moves to the same distance on the opposite side. It takes exactly two columns.

Declare them in the order `[follow, symmetric, ordering, clamp]`. Constraints run in the order you list them, so the coupling settles first, then `ordering` and `clamp` repair what it produced.

<Example
  code={aSymmetricInterval}
  title="A symmetric interval"
  blurb={"symmetric({ field: [\"lo\", \"hi\"], about: \"mean\" }) added — one end's drag mirrors the other."}
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> the low cap down: the high cap rises by the same amount. <b>Drag</b> the mean: the interval travels with it, still symmetric.
    </>
  )}
/>

</Section>

<Section id="monotonic" title="monotonic — a curve that can’t go back on itself">

A CDF, a survival function or a dose-response curve can't dip, but a drawn stroke can. `monotonic` repairs the drag: it pushes the rows the moved point would have crossed.

<Example
  code={aCurveThatOnlyRises}
  title="A curve that can only rise"
  blurb={"monotonic({ field: \"p\", along: \"year\" }) — the rule behind every CDF."}
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> a middle point up hard: the points to its right rise to meet it. Drag it down and the points to its left drop instead.
    </>
  )}
/>

A [locked](/editing/lock) row stays where it is. The dragged value stops at the nearest locked row on either side, and the push carries between them.

</Section>

<Section id="spacing" title="spacing — neighbours that keep their distance">

`spacing` keeps values a minimum distance apart. Use it where the values must stay distinct: thresholds of a scale, breakpoints of a piecewise curve, tiers of a rubric. Two points at the same value are usually a slip. Once they overlap, the top one takes every grab and you can't separate them.

<Example
  code={thresholdsThatStayApart}
  title="Thresholds that stay apart"
  blurb={"spacing({ field: \"at\", min: 8 }) — no two boundaries closer than 8 points."}
  try={(
    <>
      <b>Drag</b> one threshold into its neighbour: it pushes its neighbour, and the push carries down the line. The push keeps the values in order, so this field doesn't need <code className="inline">ordering</code> as well.
    </>
  )}
/>

The chain always fits inside the field's domain. With three thresholds beyond the one you drag, the dragged one stops `3 × min` short of the domain's edge, which leaves room for the three it pushes.

</Section>
