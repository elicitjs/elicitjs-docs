# Candidate questions for testing elicitation-interface generation

Three questions per task in `ELICITATION-TASKS.md`, 45 in all, for comparing AI agents asked to build an elicitation interface **with and without ElicitJS**. Source: `ELICITATION-QUESTIONS.json` (this file is rendered from it).

**What the agent sees.** Only the question, for example:

> Create an interface that asks people: "How old are the people in the audience at a typical classical music concert?"

The questions avoid any word that suggests a form (no *draw, drag, chart, map, slider, scale, select, mark, connect …*), so the choice of interface is entirely the agent's. Questions that need numbers to work with (fill in, summarise, explain) give them inline.

**What the agent does not see.** The task code, the frame and **Look for**. They are for the evaluator: the answer structure and coherence rules (sums, ordering, cardinality, fixed context) a good interface has to capture. Comparing the two conditions against these shows whether the agent recognised the task, not only whether it built something that works.

**Spread.** 45 domains, 9–45 words per question. Frames: 27 knowable fact (K), 9 future (F), 5 answer derivable from the given numbers (S), 4 personal or hypothetical (H).

| ID | Task | Frame | Domain | Question | Look for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| EST-1 | EST | K | Public health | What percentage of adults in your country smoke every day? | A single percentage, 0–100. |
| EST-2 | EST | K | Nutrition | How many calories are in each of these: a banana, a slice of pizza, a can of cola, a cup of cooked rice and an avocado? | One value per item (5), on a shared scale so the items can be compared. |
| EST-3 | EST | K | Architecture | How tall are the Eiffel Tower, the Empire State Building, the Burj Khalifa and the Great Pyramid of Giza? | One height per structure (4); a range wide enough for ≈ 140–830 m. |
| PRED-1 | PRED | F | Transport | How will the average price of a new electric car change between now and 2035? | A trajectory over time (one value per year or a continuous path), starting from today. |
| PRED-2 | PRED | F | Small business | A café sold 220, 240, 235 and 260 coffees on the last four Mondays. How many will it sell on each of the next four Mondays? | Four future values, with the four past values shown as given context and not editable. |
| PRED-3 | PRED + CLASS | F | Workplace | Our team has five product launches planned this year. Will each one ship on time, ship late, or be cancelled? | One of three outcomes per launch (5); a categorical forecast, not a number. |
| FILL-1 | FILL | K | Education | A school's enrolment was 480 in 2015, 505 in 2016, 560 in 2019 and 540 in 2020. The records for 2017 and 2018 were lost. What do you think enrolment was in those two years? | Two values for the missing years, set between the known years, which stay fixed. |
| FILL-2 | FILL | K | Building sensors | A sensor logged a living room's temperature every hour but went offline overnight. It read 21 °C at midnight and 18 °C at 7 a.m. What do you think the readings were from 1 a.m. to 6 a.m.? | Six hourly values bridging two fixed endpoints. |
| FILL-3 | FILL | K | Market research | In a survey of 1,000 men and 1,000 women, the men's results for last month's app use were: messaging 820, banking 640, gaming 450, fitness 310, dating 270. The women's results were lost. What do you think they were? | Five values for women (0–1,000 each) beside the men's values, which are shown and fixed. |
| UNC-1 | UNC | F | Economics | What will the inflation rate be in your country next year? Tell us how likely you think each possible value is. | A probability distribution over inflation values that sums to 1, not just a point. |
| UNC-2 | UNC + PRED | F | Infrastructure | How many years will it take to finish building the new bridge downtown? Give your best guess, and a range you are 90% sure the real answer falls in. | A best guess plus a lower and upper bound, with low ≤ best ≤ high. |
| UNC-3 | UNC | F | Technology | How likely is each of these to happen by 2030: self-driving taxis operate in most large cities; an AI-written novel becomes a bestseller; most new cars sold are electric; a human lands on Mars? | An independent probability (0–100%) per event (4); the probabilities must not be forced to sum to 100. |
| DIST-1 | DIST | K | Arts & culture | How old are the people in the audience at a typical classical music concert? | A distribution of ages (shape, bins or quantiles), not a single average. |
| DIST-2 | DIST | K | Urban life | How long does it take people in your city to get to work, and how much does that vary from person to person? | A distribution of commute times, covering both the typical value and the spread or tail. |
| DIST-3 | DIST | K | Hospitality | When people tip at a restaurant, what is a typical tip as a percentage of the bill, and how low and how high do tips usually go? | A typical value plus a low and a high (quantiles or a range) for tip percentage, ordered. |
| ALLOC-1 | ALLOC + EST | K | Student life | How does a typical university student spend the 168 hours in a week: sleeping, in class, studying, working, socialising, and everything else? | Six amounts that must add up to exactly 168 hours. |
| ALLOC-2 | ALLOC + PRED | F | Energy | Where will the world's electricity come from in 2050? | Shares across sources summing to 100%. The respondent should be able to name or add sources, since none are given. |
| ALLOC-3 | ALLOC + EST | K | Public finance | Of every dollar your national government spends, how much goes to health, education, defence, pensions, interest on debt, and everything else? | Six shares that sum to one dollar (100%). |
| REL-1 | REL | K | Consumer goods | How does the value of a used car change as it gets older? | A relationship between age and value across a range of ages; a curve, not one number. |
| REL-2 | REL | K | Learning & sleep | How does the amount of sleep a student gets the night before an exam relate to how well they score? | Score as a function of hours of sleep; the respondent should be able to express a non-linear (e.g. peaked) shape. |
| REL-3 | REL | K | Global development | Across countries, how strongly do these go together: average income, life expectancy, happiness and internet use? | A strength (and direction) for each pair of the four variables (6 pairs). |
| RANK-1 | RANK | K | Global health | Which of these kills the most people worldwide each year, and in what order do the rest follow: heart disease, road accidents, malaria, diabetes, lung cancer, HIV/AIDS? | A complete ordering of six items, with no ties unless allowed explicitly. |
| RANK-2 | RANK | H | Consumer preference | When you choose a new phone, how do these matter to you relative to each other: battery life, camera, price, screen size, brand, storage? | An order of personal importance over six features (relative weights are a bonus). |
| RANK-3 | RANK + PRED | F | Sport | In what order will these six teams finish in this season's league: Rovers, United, Athletic, City, Wanderers, Albion? | A complete predicted finishing order of six teams. |
| CLASS-1 | CLASS | K | Biology | Is each of these a mammal, bird, reptile, amphibian or fish: dolphin, penguin, bat, salamander, seahorse, platypus, eel, turtle, ostrich, newt? | One of five classes per animal (10); every animal must be assigned. |
| CLASS-2 | CLASS | K | Cybersecurity | Here are subject lines from twelve emails. Would you call each one legitimate, spam or a phishing attempt? | One of three labels per email (12). The agent has to invent plausible subject lines. |
| CLASS-3 | CLASS | K | Geography | Which climate type does each of these cities have: tropical, dry, temperate, continental or polar? Singapore, Cairo, London, Moscow, Sydney, Reykjavik, Lima, Chicago. | One of five climate types per city (8). |
| IDENT-1 | IDENT | K | Waste & recycling | Which of these can usually go in household recycling: pizza boxes, glass jars, plastic bags, aluminium cans, coffee cups, newspapers, batteries, yoghurt pots, light bulbs, cardboard? | A subset of the ten items, of any size. |
| IDENT-2 | IDENT | K | Travel | In which of these countries do people drive on the left: Japan, Brazil, India, Germany, Australia, Kenya, Canada, Thailand, Ireland, Mexico? | A subset of the ten countries. |
| IDENT-3 | IDENT + PRED | F | Future of work | Which of these jobs do you think will be largely done by machines within 15 years: cashier, nurse, truck driver, translator, electrician, accountant, teacher, radiologist, chef, call-centre agent? | A subset of the ten jobs; it can be any size, including none or all. |
| LOC-1 | LOC | K | Road safety | Where in your city do you think the most traffic accidents happen? | One or more places or areas in the respondent's own city; a location answer, not a list of street names only. |
| LOC-2 | LOC | K | Air travel | At what time of year are flights from London to New York cheapest? | One or more periods within the year (a span of dates or weeks). |
| LOC-3 | LOC | K | Medicine | Where on the body do melanomas most often appear in men, and where in women? | Body locations, given separately for men and women. |
| STRUCT-1 | STRUCT | K | Organisations | In a typical mid-sized tech company, who reports to whom among these roles: CEO, CTO, CFO, product manager, engineering manager, software engineer, designer, accountant? | A hierarchy: one parent per role, a single root, no cycles. |
| STRUCT-2 | STRUCT | K | Ecology | In a freshwater pond, what eats what among algae, snails, insects, frogs, small fish and herons? | Directed predator–prey links among six species; one species can eat or be eaten by several others. |
| STRUCT-3 | STRUCT | K | Behaviour | Why do people cancel their gym memberships? What are the reasons, and how do they influence each other? | A set of reasons the respondent names, plus directed influence relations between them. |
| SUMM-1 | SUMM + DIST | S | Community | The members of a running club are aged 23, 25, 27, 29, 31, 31, 34, 36, 38, 41, 44, 47, 52, 58 and 66. Without calculating, what is their typical age, and what range covers the middle half of the members? | A typical value plus a middle-half range, judged against the 15 ages, which are shown. |
| SUMM-2 | SUMM | S | Web analytics | A website's monthly visitors over two years (in thousands) were: 40, 38, 45, 52, 60, 71, 75, 70, 58, 50, 44, 48, 46, 44, 52, 60, 69, 80, 85, 79, 66, 57, 50, 55. Ignoring the seasonal ups and downs, what is the underlying trend? | A de-seasonalised trend over the 24 months, judged against the series, which is shown. |
| SUMM-3 | SUMM | S | Running | A runner's time for each kilometre of a 10 km race was: 4:50, 4:45, 4:46, 4:52, 4:58, 5:05, 5:01, 4:55, 4:48, 4:31. Over the course of the race, where were they speeding up and where were they slowing down? | How pace changes along the race (speeding up or slowing down by kilometre), judged from the ten splits, which are shown. |
| PREF-1 | PREF | H | Home comfort | On a winter weekday, how warm would you like your home to be at different times of the day? | A personal temperature for each time of day across 24 hours. There is no correct answer. |
| PREF-2 | PREF + ALLOC | H | Personal finance | If you received an unexpected $1,000 bonus, how would you split it between saving, paying off debt, spending, and giving? | Four amounts summing to $1,000. |
| PREF-3 | PREF | H | Mobility | How do you feel about each of these ways of getting to work: walking, cycling, bus, train, driving, working from home? | An attitude per option (6); ideally both direction (like/dislike) and strength. |
| EXPLAIN-1 | EXPLAIN + LOC | S | Retail | A shop's weekly umbrella sales last spring were: 12, 15, 11, 14, 13, 58, 61, 16, 12, 10, 14, 13. When did something unusual happen, and what do you think caused it? | The anomalous weeks (6–7) identified within the series, plus a free-text cause linked to them. |
| EXPLAIN-2 | EXPLAIN | S | Product analytics | Our app usually has about 50,000 daily active users. For three days in June it dropped to around 20,000, then recovered. What do you think explains the drop? | One or more free-text explanations; a likelihood per explanation is a bonus. |
| EXPLAIN-3 | EXPLAIN + STRUCT | K | Housing | Why have house prices in many big cities risen so much over the last decade? | The respondent's own reasons in free text, possibly with how much each contributes. |

Task codes: `EST` Estimate · `PRED` Predict · `FILL` Fill in the blanks · `UNC` Uncertainty · `DIST` Distribution · `ALLOC` Allocation · `REL` Relationship · `RANK` Rank · `CLASS` Classify · `IDENT` Identify · `LOC` Locate · `STRUCT` Structure · `SUMM` Summarise given data · `PREF` Preference · `EXPLAIN` Explain.
